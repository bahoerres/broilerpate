# WFM Animal Welfare Standards — 2024 Update

Research compiled Feb 17, 2026 from official sources.

## The Big Picture

Whole Foods has moved beyond the GAP-only approach. As of 2024, they require third-party animal welfare certification across the ENTIRE Meat department — not just major species.

**Deadline:** End of 2026 for full transition (all meat/poultry including frozen, prepackaged, heat-and-eat, smoked, cured).

## Five Approved Certifications

WFM now accepts FIVE different certification programs. All require 100+ specific welfare standards and third-party audits.

### 1. G.A.P. Animal Welfare Certified
- **Founded:** 2008
- **Structure:** Tiered — Base Level through Step 5+
- **Scale:** ~400M animals certified, 4000+ farms, 5000+ retail outlets
- **Key feature:** Step system lets farmers progress; higher step = more natural environment

**The Steps:**
- Base Level: 100+ standards, no cages/crates/crowding
- Step 2: Enrichments for natural behavior
- Step 3: Outdoor access with environmental enrichments
- Step 4: Pasture-raised, no feedlots
- Step 5: Pasture-raised, no physical alterations
- Step 5+: Entire life on same farm

### 2. A Greener World (AGW) Animal Welfare Approved
- **Launched:** 2014
- **Key feature:** STRICTEST — requires outdoor pasture/range for ENTIRE life
- **Recognition:** Only "excellent" rating from Consumer Reports for animal welfare
- **Unique:** Only U.S. label requiring audited high-welfare production, transport AND slaughter
- **Accreditation:** ISO/IEC Guide 17065 accredited

### 3. Certified Humane Raised and Handled
- **Organization:** Humane Farm Animal Care (501(c)3 nonprofit)
- **Oversight:** 40-member scientific committee (animal scientists + veterinarians)
- **Slaughter standard:** Uses Dr. Temple Grandin's NAMI standards
- **Reach:** International — U.S., Argentina, Australia, Brazil, Canada, Chile, Colombia, and more

**Core requirements:**
- No cages, crates, or tie stalls
- Animals free to do natural behaviors (dust bathing, rooting, etc.)
- Quality feed without antibiotics or growth hormones

### 4. Regenerative Organic Certified
- **Organization:** Regenerative Organic Alliance (nonprofit)
- **Structure:** Builds on USDA organic certification + three pillars:
  1. Soil health
  2. Animal welfare
  3. Social fairness
- **Key feature:** Goes beyond welfare into environmental and labor practices

### 5. RaiseWell Certified
- **Accepted at WFM:** Beef only
- **Organization:** Where Food Comes From
- **Target:** Premium meat programs, natural retailers
- **Features:** 
  - Humanely/ethically raised
  - No antibiotics ever
  - No added hormones
  - No animal by-products in feed
  - Full traceability
- **Flexibility:** Can add claims like Vegetarian Fed, Verified Grass Fed, Pasture Raised
- **Efficiency:** Can bundle with other audits (CARE Certified, etc.)

## Universal Baseline (All Five)

Every approved certification requires:
- 100+ specific animal welfare standards
- Third-party audits
- No antibiotics ever
- No added hormones
- No animal by-products in feed (cow's milk allowed for veal)
- No cages, crates, or tethers (except transport)
- Audits at slaughter
- Traceability to farm/ranch

## WFM-Specific Requirements (Beyond Certifications)

Even with third-party certification, WFM has additional requirements:
- Products labeled grass-fed must be 100% grass-fed
- Products labeled regenerative or non-GMO must be third-party verified
- No synthetic nitrates/nitrites
- No irradiation
- No transglutaminase (meat glue)
- No lean finely textured beef
- No lab-grown/cultured meat
- No GMO/BE or cloned animals
- No foie gras

## Species-Specific Notes

**Chicken:**
- Appropriate litter required for comfort/foraging
- "Complete Traceability to Farms" label means enhanced supply chain tracking
- WFM has specific broiler chicken welfare commitment

**Pork:**
- No gestation stalls
- No farrowing crates
- Bedding required in housing

**Turkey:**
- Same litter requirements as chicken
- "Complete Traceability to Farms" available

**Lamb:**
- Must be raised on pasture/range for at least 2/3 of life

**Veal:**
- Must be pasture-raised OR group housing
- No tethering, individual stalls, or crates
- Cow's milk allowed in feed
- Must have grass, forages, and/or grain

## Important Notes

- Kosher turkey/chicken cannot be animal-welfare certified (different slaughter requirements) but must still meet the 100+ welfare standards for raising
- Certification seals must appear on product packaging for transparency
